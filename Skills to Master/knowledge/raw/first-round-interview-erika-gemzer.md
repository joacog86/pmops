## Overview

Coach Erika Gemzer (200+ job seekers coached, 93% success rate at top tech companies) presents the "Minimum Viable Interview Prep" (MVIP) framework for acing first-round interviews efficiently.

## Why First-Round Interviews Exist

First-round = screening mechanism. Employer's singular objective: determine whether to invest in subsequent rounds. Typically 30-60 minutes, phone/video, one interviewer (hiring manager or peer).

Standard time allocation: 10% intros / 70% interviewer questions / 20% candidate questions.

## Four Core Preparation Pillars

### 1. Establish Your Career Narrative
- **Digital Footprint Audit:** Review all public-facing content — ensure it aligns with how you want to be seen
- **Articulate Motivations:** Avoid comp, commute, remote. Focus on what you contribute and how the role accelerates your trajectory. One decisive reason beats a list.
- **Map Job Description Language:** Create spreadsheet matching JD requirements to your experiences using the employer's exact terminology — "mirroring" creates psychological comfort

### 2. Document Major Projects (Memory Anchoring)
Instead of memorizing hundreds of answers, identify 3-5 significant recent projects (past 2-4 years). Document context, stakeholders, challenges, outcomes comprehensively.

Selection criteria: Large in scope (strategically important, many stakeholders) + Complex (multifaceted challenges requiring sophisticated problem-solving)

### 3. Master Behavioral Question Formats

**Behavioral questions = ~60% of tech interview questions**

Three formats:

**Pure Behavioral (70% of behavioral)** — "Tell me about a time when..."
STAR++ method:
- Situation: strategic context
- Task: your responsibility
- Action: your specific steps
- Result: quantified outcomes
- + Learning: lessons gained
- + Application: how you've since incorporated the learnings

**Theoretical (20% of behavioral)** — "What's your general approach to...?"
1. Outline your framework (3-5 components)
2. Explain each component with specific tactics
3. Conclude with a STAR example

**Situational (10% of behavioral)** — "Put yourself in this scenario..."
1. Ask clarifying questions
2. State assumptions explicitly
3. Outline multi-step plan with specific details
4. Verify with interviewer
5. Summarize approach

### 4. Formulate High-Signal Questions
Interviews are frequently determined by the questions YOU ask. Three-step process:
1. Identify signals you need about role viability
2. Research industry, company, competitive landscape, team
3. Craft questions showcasing homework while gathering needed information

Example (self-driving vehicle company): "In your three years at Cruise, privacy regulations have expanded with CCPA and state-level discussions — how has this impacted your work, and what's the biggest challenge as Cruise expands geographically?"

## Preparation Timeline

- Resume/LinkedIn/online presence cleanup: 1-2 hours (one-time)
- Career narrative: 1-1.5 hours
- JD analysis: 30 minutes
- Project documentation: 1-2 hours initially, 30 min refreshers
- Behavioral frameworks mastery: 4-6 hours
- Interviewer questions: 1-2 hours

**Total: 3-4 days at ~4 hrs/day, or 1 week at 2-3 hrs/day**

Much of this work transfers across multiple interviews.

## Key Insight

If you receive an offer letter immediately after first or second interviews, the employer likely didn't assess you rigorously enough — exercise caution.

## MVIP Philosophy

"Minimum Viable Interview Prep" — do the highest-leverage prep without burnout. The framework targets first-round specifically: you're not trying to ace a system design, you're trying to earn a second conversation.
