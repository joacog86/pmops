# The Product-Led Growth Flywheel

**Source:** https://www.productled.org/foundations/the-product-led-growth-flywheel  
**Ingested:** 2026-04-25  
**Author/Org:** ProductLed.org

## Introduction: From Funnel to Flywheel

The traditional sales funnel model has given way to a more dynamic approach. As products increasingly lead customer acquisition and engagement, companies are rethinking how they communicate with users and understand behavior patterns. This shift prioritizes the user experience and recognizes that "the product experience begins earlier and plays a much larger role in the user journey as a whole."

While Dave McClure's pirate metrics framework (acquisition, activation, revenue, retention, referral) served the industry well since 2007, modern SaaS businesses face new challenges. Users have heightened expectations, competition is fiercer, and traditional methodologies no longer capture the complete picture of customer relationships.

## What is the Product-Led Growth Flywheel?

The PLG Flywheel is a framework designed to generate sustainable, compounding growth through product-led user experiences. Rather than viewing the customer journey as a linear funnel, it conceptualizes growth as a cyclical process where satisfied users drive new acquisition.

The framework identifies five user segments and corresponding transition stages:

**User Segments:**
- Stranger
- Explorer
- Beginner
- Regular
- Champion

**Key Actions:**
- Evaluate
- Activate
- Adopt
- Expand
- Advocate

## Methodology

The framework emerged from interviews with over 50 companies representing diverse sizes and business models. Participants spanned marketing, sales, customer success, support, and product teams. Key findings revealed that top performers are "rethinking their approach to sales, marketing, and service in an effort to meet today's user expectations and deliver high-quality, self-service touchpoints at scale."

## Stage Breakdown

### 1. Evaluate: Stranger to Explorer

**User Profile:** Strangers browse for solutions but lack product familiarity. They conduct research across multiple competitors and channels.

**Activities include:**
- Website visits
- Content consumption
- Whitepaper downloads
- Webinar registration
- Pricing research

**What Strangers Need:**
They require clarity on what your product does, how it differentiates from alternatives, and its cost. According to research, "81% of customers research pricing and visit at least three websites before making a purchasing decision."

**Ownership:** Sales and marketing teams lead this phase, with support from product, design, and customer success teams providing market insights.

### 2. Activate: Explorer to Beginner

**User Profile:** Explorers have shown interest through free trials, freemium accounts, or calls with your team, but haven't committed long-term.

**Goals:**
Guide users to their "activation moment"—when they internalize how your product solves their problems. This represents their "time-to-value."

**Common Mistakes:**
Overwhelming users with feature tours rather than focusing on core needs. "Give them a map that shows them exactly what they need to learn to achieve their goals."

**Ownership:** Product teams typically own activation, collaborating with marketing, growth, customer success, and support functions.

### 3. Adopt: Beginner to Regular

**User Profile:** Beginners understand the product's value and feel confident it's the right solution. The adoption phase focuses on habit formation.

**Key Outcome:** Users integrate the product into regular workflows without deliberate decision-making. Using Slack as an example, adopted users choose it instinctively for communication rather than consciously selecting the tool.

**Support Needed:**
Beginners require guidance on best practices and integration into existing workflows, but with minimal friction.

**Ownership:** Product, support, and customer success teams collaborate to remove obstacles and build habits.

### 4. Expand: Regular to Champion

**User Profile:** Regular users log in frequently, have mastered core use cases, and are curious about additional problems your product can solve.

**Opportunities:**
Regular users naturally upgrade to higher-tier plans and premium features as they discover new use cases. They become candidates for expanded investments.

**Engagement Strategy:**
"Be proactive. Just because they're Regulars doesn't mean they don't need your attention." Offer educational content, exclusive feature previews, and acknowledge their importance to your business.

**Ownership:** Product, marketing, customer success, and support teams ensure delightful experiences and gather valuable feedback.

### 5. Advocate: Champion to New Strangers

**User Profile:** Champions actively invest in your product's success and would be devastated by its absence.

**Advocacy Forms:**
- Product reviews on third-party platforms
- Case study participation
- Personal recommendations
- Feature suggestions and roadmap feedback

**Activation Strategy:**
Champions often need encouragement to take action. Request reviews, case studies, or beta participation. As one expert noted, "Champions think your product is special—so be sure and let them know the feeling is mutual!"

**Ownership:** Customer success, marketing, and product teams cultivate these relationships and create advocacy opportunities.

## Real-World Examples

### Multi-Player Products: Slack

For collaborative tools, the evaluation stage often compresses. Users hear about the product and sign up immediately. They then activate through initial usage, adopt it into workflows, and expand by inviting colleagues. The true value emerges when entire teams migrate to the platform. This creates "Product-Qualified Accounts" (PQAs) that signal sales readiness.

The micro-flywheel of Activate-Adopt-Expand repeats as new invitees join, accelerating company-wide adoption.

### Viral Loop Products: Drift

Drift's free chat widget includes a "Chat ⚡️ by Drift" attribution link—an embedded advocacy mechanism. Free users skip the Expansion phase and move directly to Advocacy by displaying this link. Only paying customers access premium features and team collaboration (Expand).

### Two-Sided Marketplaces: Wise

Different user segments experience different flywheel paths. A business using Wise for international payments progresses through all five stages. Suppliers being onboarded by that business might skip Evaluate—they have no choice but to activate, adopt, and advocate to maintain client relationships.

## Key Principles

**Compounding Growth:** As users complete each stage faster, the flywheel spins more rapidly, creating exponential growth through increased advocacy.

**Flexibility:** Not all stages apply universally. The framework functions like "building blocks" that adapt to product type and user segment.

**Company-Wide Commitment:** Product-led growth requires coordination across marketing, sales, product, customer success, and support—each playing distinct roles.

**Quality Over Quantity:** Growth quality matters significantly. The framework emphasizes "ensuring users are set up for long-term success, have a truly exceptional experience, and continue to use your product to achieve their goals for years to come."

## Conclusion

The Product-Led Growth Flywheel replaces traditional siloed funnels with an interconnected system where user satisfaction and advocacy drive continuous growth. Unlike linear funnels that leak users between stages, flywheels create feedback loops where successful users attract new customers, completing the cycle.

Organizations can develop multiple flywheels—marketing-specific or sales-specific—that feed into the master PLG Flywheel. The critical takeaway: sustainable growth depends on exceptional user experiences that motivate long-term adoption and voluntary advocacy.
