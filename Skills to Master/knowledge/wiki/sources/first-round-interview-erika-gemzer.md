## TL;DR

Coach Erika Gemzer (200+ job seekers, 93% success rate at top tech companies) presents the MVIP (Minimum Viable Interview Prep) framework. The core insight: first-round interviews have one objective — earn a second conversation. Every prep decision should optimize for that, not for acing the final round.

## The MVIP Framework: Four Pillars

### 1. Career Narrative
- Digital footprint audit (align public presence with how you want to be seen)
- One decisive reason for wanting the role — not a list, not comp/commute
- Mirror the JD's language when describing your experience — psychological comfort with familiar vocabulary

### 2. Memory Anchoring via Project Documentation
Instead of memorizing hundreds of Q&As, identify 3-5 major recent projects (past 2-4 years) and document them deeply: context, stakeholders, challenges, outcomes.

Selection criteria: large scope (strategically important) + complex (multifaceted).

### 3. Behavioral Question Mastery

**Three question types:**

| Type | Frequency | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Pure Behavioral ("Tell me about a time...") | 70% of behavioral | STAR++ |
| Theoretical ("What's your approach to...") | 20% of behavioral | Framework → tactics → STAR example |
| Situational ("Put yourself in this scenario...") | 10% of behavioral | Clarify → assume → plan → verify → summarize |

**STAR++ method** (extends classic STAR):
- Situation + Task + Action + Result + **Learning** + **Application**
- The ++ is the differentiator: most candidates stop at Result. Naming what you learned and how you've applied it since signals growth mindset and self-awareness.

### 4. High-Signal Candidate Questions
Questions determine interviews as much as answers. Three-step process:
1. Identify what you genuinely need to know about role viability
2. Research industry, company, competitive landscape
3. Craft questions that show your research AND gather real information

## Prep Timeline

Total ~3-4 days at 4 hrs/day. Most of the work transfers across multiple applications — the narrative, project docs, and behavioral frameworks don't need rebuilding each time.

## Key Reframe

"Minimum Viable" is intentional. The goal of a first-round is not to demonstrate mastery — it's to reduce employer uncertainty enough to justify investing in a second conversation. Over-prepping for the final round in round one is wasted effort.
