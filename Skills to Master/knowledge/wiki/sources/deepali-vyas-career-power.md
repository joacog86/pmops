# Career Power — Agency, Positioning & Advancement — Deepali Vyas (The Elite Edge)

**Fecha:** 2025-11 – 2026-05 (6 issues) | **Fuente:** Email newsletter (eliterecruiter-newsletter@mail.beehiiv.com)

**Procesos:** `interview-prep` `strategy` `stakeholder-alignment`

## TL;DR

Performance is table stakes. The real game at senior levels is agency, visibility, influence, and sponsorship. Being excellent inside one org is not enough — you need portable evidence of value (BRAC Book), sponsors who say your name in rooms you're not in, and to operate at the next level before you have the title. The A/B/C player framework explains why B players are the biggest career blockers; the Manager→Director→VP altitude shift explains why the smartest people quietly stall.

## Core idea

**The A/B/C Player Framework:**

| Player | Drive | Operating style | Real threat? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Seeks challenge — grows under pressure | Confident self-trust, systems thinking, owns outcomes | No |
| B | Seeks credit — applause, not accountability | Calculated but insecure, protects image | Yes — blocks A talent, hires down, stalls innovation |
| C | Seeks comfort — stability, minimal disruption | Avoids visibility and risk | No |

C players aren't the real problem. **B players are.** They look competent but quietly lower the ceiling for everyone.

**The A-Player Operating System (7 patterns):**
1. Think in systems, not tasks — analyze upstream/downstream impact
2. Own outcomes, not assignments — report achievements, not activities
3. Raise the bar, not the noise — challenge the room and elevate the standard
4. Run toward problems, not recognition — volunteers into chaos, not visibility
5. Combine speed + precision — decisiveness is a performance advantage
6. Coachable at a high level — A players adapt fast; B players defend
7. Self-regulate under pressure — calm operators get trusted with more

**How to go from B→A:** Shift from image management to impact management. Say yes before you feel ready. Use BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front). Develop bias for action. Master emotional discipline. Play bigger — A players take up space unapologetically.

**The Manager→Director→VP altitude shift:**

- *Manager→Director:* Stop being indispensable to execution. Leadership asks: "Can the work happen without you?" Stop bringing answers — bring options. Stop listing tasks — explain tradeoffs. Frame enterprise impact, not team impact. If your manager left for a month and things broke, that's risk — not readiness.
- *Director→VP:* Data is incomplete, tradeoffs are unavoidable, narrative matters as much as execution. Translate activity into business outcomes. Kill ideas quickly. Articulate not just the plan — the risk you're consciously accepting. The question: "Would leadership trust you with a decision that costs millions?"

Readiness shows as **reduced friction**, not louder advocacy. If you have to convince leadership you're ready — you're not.

**BRAC System (two distinct tools, both required):**

- **BRAC File** — archive of external testimonials: emails, Slack messages, performance notes, thank-yous from clients/colleagues. Screenshot and forward everything. = Social proof.
- **BRAC Book** — quantifiable impact record. Track 2–3 entries/week: Biggest wins, Results with numbers, Accomplishments others witnessed, Contributions above your level. = Business case.

Walking into a promotion conversation with only one is like showing up to an interview with half a resume.

**The Sponsorship Gap:**

- Mentors talk *to* you. Sponsors talk *about* you.
- Sponsors don't back potential — they back patterned proof + perceived readiness.
- If your work is strong but your narrative is unclear, you feel like a risk — not a bet.
- Build sponsorship: (1) make your work legible in business outcomes, (2) engineer proximity through high-visibility cross-functional work, (3) signal readiness before the role exists, (4) give your sponsor soundbites — they can't guess your story correctly.

**The Promotion Timing Playbook:**
- Promotions are decided months before they happen. Q2 is when to act — not December.
- Meeting 1 now (Q1 wins fresh): "What would make the rest of this year a clear promotion case for me?"
- Meeting 2 in October (before budget cycles close): come with BRAC Book open — numbers, contributions above level, results others witnessed.

**The zigzag career narrative:** Find the thread (type of problem you solve, stage you thrive in) → Name the pivot with agency not circumstance → Show the compounding (each move made you better at what came after). Confidence in your own story is more persuasive than a straight line.

**Illiquid career assets and how to convert them:**
- Internal reputation → publish a point of view
- Institutional knowledge → a framework with your name on it
- Internal relationships → a network outside the org chart

Start converting now — not when you need the leverage.

## Cuándo aplicarlo

- When preparing for PM Lead interviews: the A/B/C framework tells you what the hiring panel is evaluating — pattern of ownership, systems thinking, emotional discipline under pressure — not just what you've done
- When your track record feels "inside the org": start converting illiquid assets (publish a POV, document a framework, build outside network)
- When preparing a promotion or raise conversation: use the BRAC Book as your business case, and ensure you have at least one sponsor — not just mentors

## Qué significa para Joaco

For PM Lead at N26 or similar: the Director→VP altitude shift language maps directly to what PM Lead panels evaluate — can you articulate risks consciously accepted and decisions that moved millions, not just tasks completed? Start the BRAC Book habit now, tracking quantified examples weekly. The A-player OS (systems thinking, BLUF, bias for action) is essentially the PM Lead behavioral checklist. The zigzag narrative tool is directly applicable — frame each career step as compounding evidence of PM product judgment, not a scattered path.

## Watch out

- The BRAC Book only works if updated weekly. Front-loaded habit. The system is the habit.
- Sponsorship advice assumes access to decision-makers. If you don't have it, the prior step is engineering proximity through high-visibility project work — not generic networking.
- "Operating at the next level before the title" can backfire if your current manager feels threatened. Read the political landscape before going visibly beyond scope.
