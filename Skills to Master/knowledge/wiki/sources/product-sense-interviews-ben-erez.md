## TL;DR

Ben Erez (Meta PM, serial first PM at startups) distills a five-component framework for product sense interviews based on interviewing 50+ candidates and coaching hundreds. The insight is that interviewers assess five discrete dimensions — weakness in any one fails the interview regardless of strength elsewhere.

## The Five-Component Framework

| Component | Time | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| Clear Communication | 3-5 min | Structure, waypointing, scope discipline |
| Product Motivation | 3-5 min | Connecting features to human needs + mission |
| Segmentation | 8-10 min | Non-demographic, mutually exclusive segments |
| Problem Identification | 8-10 min | Problems vs. needs, journey-grounded specificity |
| Solution Development | 8-10 min | 3+ meaningfully different ideas, V1 concreteness |

## Key Distinctions

- **Problems vs. needs:** Problems = obstacles ("struggle to find plants for limited lighting"). Needs = desires ("want beautiful flowers"). Confusing them is a top failure mode.
- **Segmentation:** Must be motivation/behavior-based, not demographic. Must be mutually exclusive — users can't belong to two segments simultaneously.
- **Product Motivation:** Move beyond features to purpose: not "share photos" but "maintain meaningful connections through visual storytelling."

## Common Failure Modes

Thinking out loud without structure. Skipping ecosystem analysis. Generic user journeys that could apply to any product. Jumping to a single solution without exploring alternatives. Creating segments that overlap.

## What This Means for Joaco

This is the canonical product sense interview framework for senior PM roles at Google/Meta/Anthropic. The five dimensions map directly to what interviewers use as a rubric — and the insight that no single dimension can compensate for another means you need to be at least competent across all five, not just excellent at one. The segmentation section is the highest-leverage skill to build: most candidates default to demographics, strong candidates segment by motivation and context.

## Tensions / Things to Interrogate

- The framework is prescriptive — real interviewers may reward genuine creative thinking over rigid structure. Balance framework adherence with natural flow.
- "20-30 practice questions" to build muscle memory is significant investment — pairs well with the Noam Segal coach's `practice` and `mock` commands.
- Ben Erez has a commercial interest (Maven bootcamp, AI Copilot) — the free guide may undersell unstructured thinking as a differentiator.
