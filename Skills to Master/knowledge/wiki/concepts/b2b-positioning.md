# B2B Positioning
**Type:** Concept / Framework

## Definition
Positioning is the act of defining how a product is uniquely valuable to a specific set of buyers relative to their alternatives. In B2B, strong positioning answers one question clearly: "Why pick us over the alternatives?" — from the perspective of the champion (the person who makes the purchase recommendation), not the product team.

April Dunford defines positioning as having five components (from *Obviously Awesome*): competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, value for the buyer, target market characteristics, and market category. Her advanced work focuses on the four roadblocks teams hit when they try to do this in practice.

## The four advanced roadblocks

**1. Disagreement about competitive alternatives**
Marketing, sales, product, and founders each see a different competitive landscape due to their different exposure points. The fix: anchor on "If we didn't exist, what would a prospect do?" — and don't forget the status quo (which accounts for ~50% of losses in B2B).

**2. Product pessimism**
Teams over-exposed to bad-fit deals convince themselves the product is undifferentiated, despite clear evidence of wins. PMs pulled into rescue-mode for poor-fit deals get a skewed view of what a good prospect looks like. Fix: focus on why you win, include experienced sales voices, stay in near-term reality.

**3. Poorly defined differentiated value**
The most common failure point. Teams stop at features, don't push to the value the champion actually understands. The "So what?" chain: feature → capability → outcome for the champion → stop before you reach "saves money" (where everyone sounds the same). Value is a reason to buy. An objection is a concern after the champion is already sold — don't confuse them.

**4. Not knowing what you're positioning**
Single-product companies don't need separate company positioning. Multi-product companies must let their GTM strategy (not internal preference) drive positioning structure: cascading, lead-product, platform/family, or by segment.

## Why it matters
In B2B, half of losses go to "no decision" — the status quo. Strong positioning wins those deals by making the value of switching clear and specific. Weak positioning (too broad, too feature-focused, too abstracted) gives buyers no reason to move.

## Key distinctions
- **Value vs. objection:** Value is why the champion puts you on the short list. Objections are barriers raised by other stakeholders after the champion is sold. Don't confuse them in your positioning.
- **Positioning vs. product strategy:** Positioning describes the product you have today for the market that exists today. It changes as the product evolves — but it never substitutes for a roadmap decision.
- **Champion vs. economic buyer:** Position for the champion first. The economic buyer has final authority but usually delegates the short list to the champion.
