# Behavioral Interview Frameworks

**Type:** Concept  
**Primary sources:** [[sources/first-round-interview-erika-gemzer]], [[sources/ai-interview-coach-lenny-noam-segal]]  
**Last updated:** 2026-04-25

## Definition

Behavioral interviews assess past performance as a predictor of future behavior. They account for ~60% of tech interview questions and are the primary format for first-round screens at top companies. Mastering them is the highest-leverage prep investment because the same stories transfer across multiple companies and roles.

## MVIP: Minimum Viable Interview Prep (Erika Gemzer)

The core philosophy: first-round interviews have one objective — earn a second conversation. Every prep decision should optimize for that, not for acing the final round.

**Four pillars:**
1. **Career Narrative** — one decisive reason for wanting the role; digital footprint aligned with how you want to be seen; mirror JD language
2. **Memory Anchoring** — 3-5 major projects documented deeply, not hundreds of Q&A pairs memorized
3. **Behavioral Question Mastery** — three formats (below)
4. **High-Signal Candidate Questions** — questions that demonstrate research and gather real info simultaneously

## Three Behavioral Question Types

| Type | Frequency | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Pure Behavioral ("Tell me about a time...") | ~70% of behavioral | STAR++ |
| Theoretical ("What's your approach to...") | ~20% of behavioral | Framework → tactics → STAR example |
| Situational ("Put yourself in this scenario...") | ~10% of behavioral | Clarify → assume → plan → verify → summarize |

## STAR++ Method (Extended STAR)

The ++ is the differentiator — most candidates stop at Result:

- **S**ituation — strategic context
- **T**ask — your specific responsibility
- **A**ction — what you did, specifically
- **R**esult — quantified outcome
- **+Learning** — what you took away
- **+Application** — how you've since used that learning

Naming Learning + Application signals growth mindset and self-awareness — two things senior PM interviewers explicitly screen for.

## Memory Anchoring

Instead of memorizing answers to hundreds of questions, build deep documentation of 3-5 projects:
- Scope: strategically important, affecting many stakeholders
- Complexity: multifaceted challenges requiring sophisticated problem-solving
- Recency: past 2-4 years

These projects become raw material for any behavioral question — the Noam Segal `stories` command operationalizes this as a story bank.

## Spiky POV > Safe Answer (Noam Segal)

From the AI interview coach framework: don't sound like everyone who prepped with AI. Lead with **earned secrets** (counterintuitive lessons from direct experience) and **spiky POVs** (specific, surprising, defensible stances). 

"I've stopped running user research before building entirely" beats "I believe in user research."

Interviewers at Google/Meta/Anthropic pattern-match on independent thinking, not framework fluency.

## Differentiation Principle

Three failure modes (Noam Segal research):
1. **Impostor spiral** — no feedback loop, can't diagnose if it's skills, resume, or luck
2. **Blind grind** — effort with no signal it's aimed correctly
3. **Practice gap** — can't improve something you do once every few years

AI closes the loop: paste transcripts → get line-by-line feedback → iterate.
