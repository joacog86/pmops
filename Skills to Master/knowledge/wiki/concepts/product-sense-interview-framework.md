# Product Sense Interview Framework

**Type:** Concept  
**Primary source:** [[sources/product-sense-interviews-ben-erez]]  
**Last updated:** 2026-04-25

## Definition

A structured 5-component framework for product sense (PS) interviews — the most common senior PM interview format at top-tier companies. Unlike behavioral interviews, PS interviews ask you to design or improve a product from scratch, and evaluate whether you think like a PM who ships things users want.

## The Five Components (Ben Erez Framework)

| # | Component | Time | Core test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear Communication | 3-5 min | Structure before diving in; waypointing; scope discipline |
| 2 | Product Motivation | 3-5 min | Features → human needs → mission; not "what" but "why" |
| 3 | Segmentation | 8-10 min | Motivation/behavior-based, mutually exclusive segments |
| 4 | Problem Identification | 8-10 min | Journey-grounded friction; problems vs. needs distinction |
| 5 | Solution Development | 8-10 min | 3+ meaningfully different ideas; V1 definition; risk mitigation |

**Critical rule:** Excellence in one dimension cannot compensate for weakness in another. Interviewers assess all five independently.

## Key Distinctions

**Problems vs. needs:** A problem is an obstacle ("can't find plants that work for low light"). A need is a desire ("want a beautiful indoor garden"). Naming problems correctly directs solution thinking.

**Segmentation:** Segments must be (a) motivation/behavior-based, not demographic, and (b) mutually exclusive — if a user can belong to two segments simultaneously, the segmentation is broken.

**Product Motivation:** Move beyond feature description. "Share photos" → "enable people to maintain meaningful connections through visual storytelling." The mission framing guides every downstream decision.

## Opening Move (High-Leverage)

Before any analysis: "Before I dive in, I'd like to walk you through my plan for our time together..." then state your steps explicitly and ask "Does this plan sound good to you?" This signals structure, respects the interviewer's time, and creates checkpoints.

## Common Failure Modes

- Thinking out loud without visible structure
- Demographics-only segmentation ("18-35 year olds")
- Generic user journeys that could describe any product
- Single solution without exploring alternatives
- Confusing needs ("want X") with problems ("can't do Y")
- Jumping to solutions before problems are well-defined

## Relationship to Other Interview Types

PS interviews test product intuition and user empathy. They pair with:
- **Analytical Thinking interviews** (→ [[concepts/analytical-thinking-interview-framework]]) — more metric-focused, less design-focused
- **Behavioral interviews** (→ [[concepts/behavioral-interview-frameworks]]) — about past experience, not product design

## Prep Approach

Build muscle memory through 20-30 practice questions. Time each section. Practice waypointing out loud — it feels unnatural at first but signals to interviewers that you're organized. Use the Noam Segal coach's `mock` command for full simulations and `analyze` for transcript scoring.
